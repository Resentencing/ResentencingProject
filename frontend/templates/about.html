About Us

Who we are, who this is for, and how we handle public data responsibly.

This project is a collaboration between students, researchers, legal advocates, legal practitioners, formerly incarcerated people, and others. We work to make resentencing data more understandable and accessible. The team brings together experience in law, data analysis, and technology to study how CDCR-initiated resentencing works in practice.

This service provides information and research tools only and does not provide legal advice. The system uses artificial intelligence to generate responses based on available data, and while efforts are made to ensure accuracy, AI outputs may contain errors or incomplete information. Users should independently verify all information before relying on it for legal, research, or decision-making purposes.

Our Mission

Our mission is to increase transparency in the resentencing process and make complex legal data accessible and usable for people who need it. We aim to support fair decision-making by helping users understand how cases move through the system and what factors are associated with outcomes.

Our Vision

We believe sentencing letters and data should not be hidden from people. Our goal is to create tools that allow people to explore, understand, and use this information to support research, advocacy, and legal work. We hope this work contributes to the reduction of the prison population and to a more transparent and equitable system.

The Problem We Are Solving

Resentencing decisions are documented in public records, but those records are hard to access and even harder to use. Information is spread across PDF letters, spreadsheets, and separate datasets that are not connected. As a result:

  • it is difficult to understand how decisions are made
  • it is hard to compare cases or identify patterns
  • important information remains effectively inaccessible

This project organizes and connects that information so users can see both individual cases and system-wide patterns.

Who This Is For

We designed this system for:

  • lawyers and legal workers working on resentencing cases
  • researchers studying the legal system
  • advocates, formerly incarcerated people, family members, and stakeholders trying to understand cases
  • policymakers interested in how the system operates and improving it

Some users may explore patterns across many cases, while others may look closely at a single case.

Why We Restrict Some Data Even Though It Is Public

All of the data used in this project comes from public records. We believe that not all public data should be made easily accessible without limits. Some records include personally identifying information and sensitive details about individuals' lives, including:

  • criminal history and sentencing details
  • prison discipline and behavior records
  • participation in programs and education
  • medical and mental health information
  • family relationships and support systems

Even though this information is technically public, making it easily searchable and downloadable creates risks: data could be scraped or copied in bulk; information could be reposted or misused; individuals could be harmed while incarcerated or after release; and AI can misuse it.

To reduce these risks, access to detailed case-level data is limited. Login systems help monitor use and prevent misuse, while still allowing researchers, legal professionals, and advocates to access the information responsibly.
